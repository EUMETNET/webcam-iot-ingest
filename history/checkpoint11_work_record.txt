Checkpoint 11 work record - Skaping ingestion
==============================================

Status
------

Implemented, fixture-tested, and bounded-live-tested on 2026-07-23. Owner
acceptance remains pending.

Freshness choice
----------------

Skaping ingestion uses the registered point of view's `latest_media.mini`
pointer. A followed HEAD request transfers no image body and obtains the final
object's ETag and `Last-Modified` headers. The ETag is the opaque freshness
marker. A changed object is downloaded from the resolved URL and accepted only
if the GET response repeats the checked ETag, preventing a HEAD/GET race.

`Last-Modified` is converted to UTC and carried as provider availability time
through N0V0 and latency metrics. It is not described as physical capture
time. No timestamp is parsed from the undocumented object path.

The documented archive method was not implemented. In the bounded comparison,
it returned a full 24-hour mixed image/video history, sometimes tens of
kilobytes per camera. The point-of-view HEAD method succeeded for all 41 active
streams with zero response-body bytes and 0.126 seconds mean duration. Eight of
nine comparable archive responses matched; one archive was five minutes ahead.

Pipeline and scheduling
-----------------------

Changed mini images reuse shared validation, T0V0 transformation, immutable S3
storage, durable publication outbox, N0V0 MQTT QoS 1 publication, adaptive
polling, and deferred EMA behavior. The shared due-selection function retains
its provider-marker timestamp strategy as the default used by Windy and
Fintraffic. Skaping alone requests the `download_timestamp` strategy:

    last_freshness_query_timestamp + minimum_ingestion_interval <= now

and, after at least one successful download:

    last_download_timestamp
      + max(minimum_ingestion_interval, lambda * ema_download_period) <= now

The first guard also applies when no download has ever succeeded. A HEAD or
download failure therefore records its freshness attempt and cannot be
selected again for 300 seconds, even though the ETag remains unprocessed.
This experiment needs no provider-timestamp schema column and cannot change
Windy or Fintraffic behavior unless their call sites explicitly opt in.

Skaping-specific freshness and download retry counts default independently to
zero. The continuous worker exposes the same bounded metric vocabulary under
`source_network="ska"` on port 8015, and the dedicated Grafana dashboard
filters every query accordingly.

Validation evidence
-------------------

The focused ingestion, metrics, storage, notification, worker, and
configuration suite passed 70 tests with one optional test skipped. Thirteen
real-PostgreSQL registry integration tests then passed, including explicit
coverage of the new anchor, never-successful failure backoff, invalid-anchor
rejection, and the unchanged default provider-marker policy. The bounded live
publication test
selected four streams and published all four successfully through validation,
T0V0, EWC S3, durable outbox, and MQTT. Source images were JPEG/RGB and their
ETags were stored as provider markers.

A separate four-stream dry worker epoch completed in 7.221 seconds and exposed
Skaping-labelled database, provider-refresh, job, download, image, and latency
metrics from its `/metrics` endpoint. It made no S3 or MQTT writes.

One-hour benchmark
------------------

The accepted full-network command ran for one hour with deterministic
staggering and completed 240 epochs and 430 jobs: 185 images were published
and 245 ETags were unchanged. No freshness-query, download, transformation,
S3, MQTT, or throttling failure was observed. Epochs were normally about one
to two seconds; the maximum isolated duration was 6.515 seconds.

Over the final 30 minutes, Prometheus measured approximately 37 checks and 14
complete publications per five minutes, 0.00188 MB/s of external image
payload, and 0.00250 MB/s of successful S3 PUT payload. These results and the
corresponding latency, EMA, and image-profile percentiles are presented in
`docs/skaping-ingestion.html`.
