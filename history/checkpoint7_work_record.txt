Checkpoint 7 work record - operational Windy worker
===================================================

Post-checkpoint note (2026-07-30): the durable publication outbox and replay
mechanism documented below were removed by the first code review after
checkpoint 12. Delivery is now synchronous and ambiguous retries may produce
duplicates. The remainder records what checkpoint 7 validated at the time.

Status
------

Nearly finished and undergoing repository-owner performance validation on
2026-07-23. Checkpoint 7 is not yet marked accepted; do not begin Fintraffic
until the owner accepts the validation described below.

Current differences from the architecture background
----------------------------------------------------

Checkpoint 7 currently has three deliberate implementation refinements with
respect to the architecture background used to guide the repository:

1. Due selection uses two per-stream time guards: time must be later than the
   last successful download plus the minimum ingestion interval, and later
   than the stored provider timestamp plus `0.7 * ema_download_period`.
2. EMA updates are deferred until the end of the epoch and applied as one
   conditional database batch only when the epoch is not the first epoch and
   its duration is below the minimum ingestion interval. Other epochs discard
   their candidates, while download and durable publication state remain
   immediate.
3. Observability adds three latency measures derived from the provider update
   timestamp: provider update to successful download, successful download to
   completed MQTT job, and their sum. For Windy, `lastUpdatedOn` is the
   provider marker known at metadata refresh rather than a guaranteed physical
   acquisition timestamp for the downloaded bytes. The measures therefore
   estimate provider-marker age at ingestion and internal completion latency.

Implemented scope
-----------------

- `python -m ingestion.worker --network win` provides a continuous foreground
  worker with a bounded thread pool, configurable idle/failure delays, country
  and job limits, bounded `--epochs` validation runs, and duration-bounded
  `--run-for-seconds` benchmarks that stop new intake at the deadline and
  finish the active epoch.
- Epoch starts are separated by at least 15 seconds by default. The benchmark
  recipe uses zero additional idle delay.
- Migration `003_adaptive_polling.sql` adds
  `last_freshness_query_timestamp`. For streams with prior successful state,
  both the last-download minimum-interval guard and the provider-timestamp EMA
  guard must pass. Streams without a prior download remain immediately
  eligible. Every applied Windy freshness attempt is recorded, including
  provider errors, while dry runs remain non-mutating.
- `last_freshness_query_timestamp` is deliberately not a third eligibility
  guard in the current owner-requested experiment. Once both time guards pass,
  an unchanged or provider-error stream can therefore be selected again in
  the next epoch until a successful download advances its stored state. The
  resulting request load is part of the checkpoint performance measurement.
- Windy metadata request starts remain serialized through a shared rate gate
  even when image jobs use multiple worker threads.
- Windy metadata/freshness requests and JPEG downloads now have independent,
  network-scoped retry controls. `WINDY_FRESHNESS_QUERY_RETRY_COUNT` and
  `WINDY_DOWNLOAD_RETRY_COUNT` both default to zero retries; an experiment can
  explicitly set either value to one or more. `RETRY_BACKOFF_S` remains their
  shared backoff duration and defaults to one second. A freshness retry repeats
  the complete metadata/reference
  operation, including HTTP access, JSON validation, provider-ID and marker
  validation, and selected-rendition lookup. A download retry applies only to
  the later JPEG request. The former generic `DOWNLOAD_RETRY_COUNT` setting is
  no longer used for Windy.
- `last_download_timestamp`, the provider marker, and durable publication
  outbox state remain immediate and atomic. EMA is the only deferred field:
  worker threads return candidates to the epoch coordinator, which applies
  them in one database transaction only when the epoch is not epoch 1 and its
  duration is below `MINIMUM_INGESTION_INTERVAL_S`. Long epochs discard all
  their candidates. Conditional updates require the candidate download
  timestamp still to be current, preventing a stale epoch from overwriting a
  newer worker update. This deliberately preserves restart/outbox consistency
  instead of deferring the authoritative download timestamp for a negligible
  reduction in the existing publication transaction.
- SIGINT and SIGTERM stop new epoch intake and allow orderly worker shutdown.
- `/livez`, `/readyz`, and `/metrics` are served on the configured health
  address. Metrics use controlled network, stage, and outcome labels rather
  than camera or image identifiers.
- Per-job observability includes source-job duration and outcome; controlled
  stage failure reasons; transformation version, outcome, duration, derived
  image count, byte size, width, and height; S3 and MQTT outcomes and
  durations; and built MQTT payload size. Successful complete publications
  emit `provider_to_download`, `download_to_job_end`, and their sum
  `provider_to_job_end`. Histograms and counters use only bounded labels, and
  the Grafana worker dashboard exposes the latency and job-duration series.
- Windy metadata timing is split into `provider_rate_gate_wait` (controlled
  local request-start pacing), `provider_http` (HTTP operation including its
  configured retries), and `provider_refresh` (the compatible end-to-end sum,
  including response parsing). This prevents a queue of quickly completing
  unchanged jobs from being misread as increased Windy API response latency.
- Docker Compose includes the optional `worker` profile and Prometheus scrapes
  the worker metrics endpoint.
- `just ingestion-test <scope> <duration> staggered` assigns each source stream
  a reproducible SHA-256-derived first-check phase. The fixed seed is
  `windy-benchmark-v1`; `INITIAL_STAGGER_WINDOW_S` defaults to 600 seconds and
  is independent of EMA. This state is in memory only and does not invent
  registry timestamps. Each stream's first staggered EMA candidate is
  discarded as cold-start evidence.
- The Grafana dashboard shows cumulative epoch-completion steps, one-minute
  instantaneous job/outcome rates, five-minute smoothed completed-job and
  published-image throughput, five-minute stage means and p50/p95 histograms,
  and explicit information-tool-tip descriptions of every window.

Retry and publication recovery
------------------------------

The configured defaults are zero transformation retries, zero Windy freshness
retries, zero Windy image-download retries, one S3 upload retry, and one MQTT
publication retry. Publication retries reuse the same transformed bytes,
image identifier, immutable object key, and notification payload.

Checkpoint 7 adds `publication_outbox` as an explicitly documented operational
extension to the original three-table pilot registry. Successful image download
state and the pending publication are recorded atomically. The worker drains
pending publications before selecting new images. Its durable stages are:

- `pending_s3`: transformed bytes and the notification are retained until the
  immutable S3 object is confirmed;
- `pending_mqtt`: S3 has succeeded and only MQTT publication remains;
- completion: the MQTT QoS 1 publish is acknowledged and the outbox row is
  removed.

S3 replay does not rely on `IfNoneMatch`, whose support was uncertain on the
EWC S3-compatible service. It performs `HEAD` before `PUT`, stores a SHA-256
digest as object metadata, accepts an existing object only when size and digest
match, and refuses to overwrite different content. Database outbox ownership
is the normal concurrency guard; the S3 comparison is the restart safeguard.

Validation evidence
-------------------

The focused offline suite was rerun on 2026-07-21:

    env UV_CACHE_DIR=/tmp/webcam-uv-cache uv run --env-file .env pytest -q \
      tests/ingestion tests/transformation tests/storage tests/notification \
      tests/worker tests/metrics tests/test_deployment_config.py

Result: 48 passed and 1 skipped in 0.69 seconds. The skipped health socket test
is conditional for restricted sandboxes; it had previously passed when run
with local socket permission.

Database schema and registry integration tests completed with 16 passing tests
in 25.22 seconds after applying migrations `002_publication_outbox.sql` and
`003_adaptive_polling.sql` to the existing PostgreSQL volume. The current
selection test covers the conjunction of the last-download minimum-interval
guard and the provider-timestamp-plus-EMA guard.

A bounded live dry run used DK, two jobs per epoch, and two epochs. Both epochs
selected and downloaded exactly two images. A live health exercise reported
`live` and `ready`, exposed the expected epoch/job metrics, and exited cleanly
after SIGINT.

Durable replay was tested with one DK image by deliberately pointing S3 at a
closed local endpoint. The first delivery ended in `storage_error` and left one
`pending_s3` row containing 16,374 derived bytes with one recorded failed
delivery cycle. Running `python -m ingestion.replay_publications --limit 1`
against EWC then uploaded the exact object, published N0V0 to the local MQTT
broker, and removed the outbox row. S3 `HEAD` confirmed a 16,374-byte JPEG with
the SHA-256 metadata. MQTT received the expected N0V0 payload.

A full France exercise subsequently covered all 2,056 registered streams:
2,038 (99.12%) completed successfully and 18 (0.88%) persistently returned
provider errors. No HTTP 429 throttling occurred with 100 threads and a
0.01-second Windy request-start interval. At the new 15-second minimum epoch
period, those 18 failures caused at most 1.2 freshness requests per second
under the then-current no-retry configuration. A five-minute revisit also found
72 new images among the earlier 198-camera sample and published all of them.

A three-epoch France+Germany adaptive-polling run on 2026-07-22 selected 4,849,
4,849, and 4,847 streams. Epoch durations were 95.43, 65.57, and 55.49
seconds. Published outcomes were 4,710, 2,322, and 1,485; no throttling was
observed. Epoch 1 discarded its 4,710 EMA candidates as designed, while epochs
2 and 3 conditionally applied 2,322 and 1,485 candidates. The run also exposed
that the original `provider_refresh` duration combined local rate-gate waiting
with HTTP latency, leading to the explicit timing split documented above.

A 100-minute all-Windy benchmark completed on 2026-07-23 with the deterministic
initial phase spread over 600 seconds. Prometheus retained the complete
pipeline metrics. For the final 30-minute steady window, normalized averages
per five minutes were 18,615 completed camera checks, 10,271 successful image
downloads, 10,271 transformations, 10,269 S3 uploads, and 10,269 completed
MQTT publications. Provider-marker-unchanged outcomes averaged 7,849 and
provider-access errors 497 per five minutes; no throttling series was present.
The provider-marker-to-completed-MQTT histogram estimated p50 at 2.31 minutes
and p95 at 5.95 minutes, while download-to-completed-MQTT p95 was 0.98 seconds.
These are Prometheus bucket estimates for 06:48:20--07:18:20 UTC, not exact
order statistics.

After that benchmark, the approximately 497 provider-access errors per five
minutes were identified as failures of the Windy metadata/reference operation,
before JPEG download. Such a failure prevents publication in the current epoch
and may add an epoch to the eventual provider-marker-to-download delay. The two
Windy-specific retry settings were therefore introduced and tested with one
immediate retry each. Their operational defaults were then restored to zero
after the retry run showed a worse latency tail despite fewer provider errors.
Future controlled benchmarks can explicitly enable them. Persistent provider
failures incur one additional request and the configured backoff only when a
retry count is nonzero. Image-download errors remain separately observable.

Explicit-ID batch freshness experiment
--------------------------------------

The isolated `codex-windy-batched-freshness` branch adds an opt-in
Fintraffic-style epoch snapshot. After due-job selection and optional initial
staggering, the worker partitions the exact registered provider IDs into
groups of at most 50 and requests Windy listings with the `webcamIds` filter
and `include=images`. It does not use discovery countries or geographic discs.
Each response supplies `lastUpdatedOn` and the current rendition URL; ordinary
source jobs then read those references from the in-memory snapshot. An omitted
ID becomes a controlled provider error for that epoch.

The individual per-camera refresh remains the default. The alternative is
enabled with `--batch-freshness`, or with the `batched` and
`staggered-batched` just modes. Existing `provider_refresh` and `provider_http`
metrics retain the same stage meanings as Fintraffic. Additional bounded
metrics count batch requests, returned and missing streams, and batch sizes.

A complete read-only probe over the pre-discovery registry selected 25,061
due streams and resolved them with 502 successful requests in 5.377 seconds
using 20 threads. It returned 24,551 references and omitted 510 IDs; a bounded
comparison found that omitted IDs were unavailable from individual endpoints
as well. A subsequent full discovery inactivated 519 old streams, supporting
that interpretation. Live worker dry runs after discovery resolved 43 of 43
Denmark streams in one batch and 200 of 200 France streams in four batches.
Their complete image-validation epochs took 0.685 and 2.100 seconds,
respectively, with no missing, failed, or throttled request.

Provider timestamp visibility diagnostic
----------------------------------------

On 2026-07-31, the isolated `codex-windy-visibility-probe` branch tested
whether Windy exposes a changed `lastUpdatedOn` substantially later than the
timestamp itself. A deterministic read-only sample contained 1,000 active
Windy streams whose learned EMA was at most 600 seconds. Batched metadata was
queried every 15 seconds; the first snapshot established a baseline, and the
probe performed neither image downloads nor database writes.

After 35 cycles, 631 later marker changes had been observed without a provider
error. The first-visibility delay was approximately 40 seconds at p50, 67
seconds at p90, and 70 seconds at p95, with an observed range of 7--84 seconds.
The 15-second sampling cadence and batch completion time make these upper-bound
estimates rather than exact publication instants. They indicate that provider
visibility explains roughly one minute of latency, but not the then-current
2.60-minute median provider-timestamp-to-download gap. Adaptive polling, EMA
overestimation, or scheduling therefore remained necessary follow-up causes.
The experimental implementation and protocol are retained in local commit
`133b6a4` on that branch.

For the next controlled Windy benchmark, the Windy-only minimum publication
interval and initial EMA were reduced from 300 to 120 seconds, and the polling
factor from 0.7 to 0.5. Fintraffic and Skaping retain their validated shared
defaults. The purpose is to test whether the remaining provider-to-download
latency is primarily produced by an overestimated Windy EMA and its polling
guard; this is an experimental checkpoint-7 refinement, not yet a final
production conclusion.

Checkpoint decision
-------------------

Checkpoint 7 is accepted. Its validation procedure remains in
`manual_tests/validation_to_complete_checkpoint7`; the later retry experiment
and restored zero-retry defaults are recorded above. Checkpoint 8 Fintraffic
discovery was subsequently implemented and accepted.
