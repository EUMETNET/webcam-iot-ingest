Work between checkpoints 12 and 13 - Windy polling optimization
================================================================

Date: 2026-08-01

Status and purpose
------------------

This work was performed after checkpoint 12 and before the checkpoint 13
recovery and operational drills. Its purpose was to reduce Windy freshness
traffic and provider-to-download latency before running the three ingestion
networks together in a quiet full-system baseline.

The experiments kept the main execution conditions comparable: the complete
Windy registry, batched freshness requests, 100 worker threads, deterministic
ten-minute initial staggering, a five-minute per-stream successful-publication
guard, a fifteen-second minimum epoch period, and two hours of execution.

Redefinition of the former EMA
------------------------------

The old `ema_download_period` quantity was originally an exponentially
weighted estimate derived from effective download gaps. That interpretation
was too sensitive to missed observations: one provider or acquisition gap
could inflate the estimate and defer later freshness checks, which could in
turn reinforce the overestimate.

For Windy, the quantity is now understood conceptually as the estimated source
stream period. The tested estimator is initialized as `NULL` and learns from
differences between successive distinct Windy provider timestamps. Once an
estimate exists, it keeps the bounded running minimum of the prior estimate
and the newly observed provider-timestamp gap, with a five-minute lower bound
against irregular or invalid gaps. The update remains deferred to the end of
an eligible epoch and preserves the existing first-epoch and overlong-epoch
guards.

Immediately after these experiments, migration 007 renamed the shared database
column from the legacy `ema_download_period` name to
`estimated_source_stream_period` without changing any stored value. The neutral
name is shared by all networks, while their estimators and polling floors remain
network-specific.

Experiments and results
-----------------------

The first adaptive run used the redefined bounded-minimum estimate and lambda
0.7. The control disabled adaptive selection but continued learning the same
estimate. The final run restored lambda 0.7 and imposed the polling guard:

    max(9 minutes, 0.7 * estimated source-stream period)

The nine-minute value is a polling-interval floor. It neither overwrites nor
clamps the stored period estimate. The independent five-minute
successful-publication guard remains unchanged.

Interpretation and retained threshold
-------------------------------------

Removing adaptive polling increased selected jobs and provider requests by
about 11.5 percent without increasing publication throughput or improving
latency. Adaptive selection is therefore useful.

After the first experiments, 24,391 of 25,043 active Windy streams had an
estimate. The mean was 16.91 minutes and P05 was 10 minutes. There were 591
estimates at the explicit five-minute estimator floor, none strictly between
five and 7.5 minutes, and the next value was 599 seconds. This distribution is
compatible with the shorter gaps being provider irregularities rather than a
stable refresh period, although it does not prove their cause.

Compared with adaptive polling without a polling floor, the nine-minute floor
reduced selected jobs and provider requests by 31.7 percent while publishing
0.8 percent more images. Whole-run P95 latency improved from 7.22 to 6.14
minutes. More importantly for the steady part of the run, final-window P95
remained below five minutes and improved from 4.97 to 4.78 minutes. This means
the threshold helps the operational P95 remain below five minutes; it does not
guarantee that every individual image is downloaded within five minutes.

The nine-minute polling floor with lambda 0.7 is retained as the most efficient
tested Windy setting before optimizing the quiet three-network run. Provider
requests fell substantially, publication throughput was preserved, no
throttling occurred, and the latency objective was maintained. Synchronized
provider waves remain visible in the P90 epoch duration and should be observed
during the upcoming three-network baseline rather than addressed with another
Windy-only scheduling change at this stage.
